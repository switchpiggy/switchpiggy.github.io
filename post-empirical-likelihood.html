The one idea

Empirical likelihood replaces a parametric model with the nonparametric family of distributions supported on the data. Given samples $x_1,\dots,x_n$ and weights $w_i \ge 0$, we maximize the profile ratio

$$ R(\theta) \;=\; \max\left\{ \prod_{i=1}^{n} n\,w_i \;:\; \sum_i w_i = 1,\ \sum_i w_i\, g(x_i,\theta) = 0 \right\}. $$

The constraint $\sum_i w_i\, g(x_i,\theta)=0$ is where your estimating equation lives. Everything interesting is a consequence of how the weights redistribute mass to satisfy it.

Why it shows up in TRUST

Because $-2\log R(\theta)$ is asymptotically $\chi^2$, you get confidence regions with no variance estimate and no Gaussian assumption — which is exactly what you want when the thing you're quantifying uncertainty over is a neural network.

The shape of the region is chosen by the data, not by you. That's the selling point and the catch.

A snippet

def el_ratio(g, theta):
    # solve the dual for the Lagrange multiplier lambda
    lam = solve_dual(g, theta)
    w = 1.0 / (n * (1 + lam @ g))
    return np.prod(n * w)
